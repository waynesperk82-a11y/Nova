# LhamaBot public-information review

**Reviewed:** 7 November 2026  
**Purpose:** A cautious product/security benchmark for NOVA. This is not an endorsement, investment recommendation, or proof of the service's legitimacy.

## Executive assessment

The available public material is not enough to classify LhamaBot as either a scam or a verified trustworthy service. Its website is reachable and publishes legal/risk terms, and one automated website scanner reported no major malware/phishing detections. Those signals do **not** verify the operator's identity, the safety of its financial operations, the accuracy of performance claims, or the ability of customers to withdraw funds.

The screenshots' performance claims (111 cycles, 108 profitable, 97.3% success, +76.50 USDT) are promotional claims. No independently reproducible account statement, complete trade ledger, third-party audit, or verified withdrawal evidence was found in this review.

## What the public material says

- The platform describes itself as an automation layer for digital-asset operations, including spot/futures strategies and tokenised US-stock exposure.
- Its legal page says it is not a broker or custodian; execution occurs through connected broker accounts or user-controlled wallets.
- The same page claims API keys are encrypted and says it does not ask for broker passwords or wallet recovery phrases. These are operator claims, not an independent security audit.
- The legal page discloses risks including execution failures, venue outages, fees, funding, spread changes, and sudden market moves.
- Its terms state that account holders must be at least 18 years old.
- The fee model is described as charges per completed operation and account top-ups; the exact current fees should be checked in the published terms before any adult user considers the service.

## Independent signals and limitations

- Gridinsoft's automated scan reported no major malware/phishing detections and gave the domain a 78/100 trust score. This is a website/infrastructure signal only; it is not a financial, legal, custody, or performance audit.
- SiteConfiavel reported no Reclame Aqui profile at the time of its check and described the domain as registered about four months earlier (22 October 2026).
- Gridinsoft reported an apparent registration date of 16 October 2018 and eight years of domain history, while also noting that registrant ownership was not publicly available. These domain-age reports conflict and were not resolved through a successful authoritative WHOIS lookup in this review.
- No independently verified withdrawal track record, complete audited performance report, named legal operator, or independently confirmed CNPJ was established from the material reviewed.

## Conclusion

**Status: unverified — proceed with caution.** A valid HTTPS certificate, a working website, a clean malware scan, and a self-published legal page do not prove that a financial automation service is safe or profitable. Equally, the evidence reviewed does not establish that it is fraudulent.

The 18+ age requirement in the platform's own terms is explicit. This review is for product research only, not account access or use.

## NOVA product lessons

1. **Keep market connections read-only by default.** Do not expose broker order placement or wallet signing in the current prototype.
2. **Show provenance for every number.** Store provider, source timestamp, retrieval time, symbol, currency, and whether a figure is live, delayed, simulated, or manually entered.
3. **Separate verified facts from operator claims.** Label self-reported performance as unverified unless independently reconciled against complete source records.
4. **Never present win rate alone.** If a future educational simulator reports results, include fees, funding, slippage assumptions, open positions, drawdown, sample period, and the complete calculation method.
5. **Use least-privilege integrations.** Secrets stay server-side; use read-only scopes where possible; never request withdrawal permissions, passwords, or wallet recovery phrases.
6. **Add a risk and approval layer.** Display clear risk notices, audit all data changes, require explicit human approval for any future external action, and keep simulation data visibly separate from live data.
7. **Avoid performance promises.** Historical or simulated results must never be described as guaranteed future outcomes.
